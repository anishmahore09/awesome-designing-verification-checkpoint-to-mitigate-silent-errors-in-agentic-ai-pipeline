# Verified Research Papers

This collection contains verified scholarly research papers related to designing verification checkpoints to mitigate silent errors in Agentic AI pipelines. The papers are organized by research theme.

## 1. Survey and Review Papers

- **A Survey of Large Language Models**
  Zhao et al., 2023, arXiv.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)
  Comprehensive survey covering LLM architectures, capabilities, evaluation, and limitations.

- **A Survey on Evaluation of Large Language Models**
  Chang et al., 2024, ACM Transactions on Intelligent Systems and Technology.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03109)
  Reviews methods and benchmarks for evaluating LLM reliability and capabilities.

- **Large Language Models as Agents: A Survey**
  Wang et al., 2024, arXiv.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07864)
  Surveys the use of LLMs as autonomous agents and their interaction with tools and environments.

## 2. Foundational Research

- **Attention Is All You Need**
  Vaswani et al., 2017, NeurIPS.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)
  Introduced the Transformer architecture underlying modern language models.

- **Language Models are Few-Shot Learners**
  Brown et al., 2020, NeurIPS.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)
  Demonstrated strong few-shot capabilities of large language models.

- **Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback**
  Ouyang et al., 2022, NeurIPS.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)
  Established RLHF as an important approach for improving instruction following and alignment.

- **Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models**
  Wei et al., 2022, NeurIPS.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903)
  Demonstrated that prompting models to generate reasoning steps can improve complex reasoning performance.

- **ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models**
  Yao et al., 2023, ICLR.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)
  Combines reasoning and tool/action execution, providing a foundation for agentic workflows.

## 3. Verification and Self-Checking

- **Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models**
  Wang et al., 2023, ICLR.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)
  Uses multiple reasoning paths to improve reliability of generated answers.

- **Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback**
  Madaan et al., 2023, NeurIPS.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)
  Introduces iterative self-feedback and refinement for improving model outputs.

- **Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback**
  Bai et al., 2022, Anthropic.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073)
  Explores AI-generated feedback for improving model behavior and reducing undesirable outputs.

- **Let's Verify Step by Step**
  Lightman et al., 2023, ICLR.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)
  Shows the value of process-level verification for mathematical reasoning.

- **Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models**
  Dhuliawala et al., 2024, ACL.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495)
  Proposes generating verification questions and checking answers to reduce hallucinations.

## 4. Agentic AI Reliability and Evaluation

- **AgentBench: Evaluating LLMs as Agents**
  Liu et al., 2023, ICLR.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03688)
  Provides a benchmark for evaluating LLM-based agents across different environments.

- **SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?**
  Jimenez et al., 2024, ICLR.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770)
  Evaluates AI agents on real-world software engineering tasks.

- **GAIA: A Benchmark for General AI Assistants**
  Mialon et al., 2024, ICLR.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12983)
  Evaluates general-purpose AI assistants on questions requiring reasoning, tools, and multimodal information.

- **τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains**
  Yao et al., 2024, arXiv.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)
  Evaluates agents interacting with tools and users in realistic tasks.

## 5. Tool-Using and Multi-Agent Systems

- **Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools**
  Schick et al., 2023, NeurIPS.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)
  Demonstrates how language models can learn to call external tools.

- **AutoGen: Enabling Next-Gen LLM Applications via Multi-Agent Conversation**
  Wu et al., 2023, arXiv.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08155)
  Presents a framework for building applications through conversations between multiple AI agents.

- **CAMEL: Communicative Agents for Mind Exploration of Large Scale Language Model Society**
  Li et al., 2023, NeurIPS Workshop.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17760)
  Explores role-playing and communication between multiple autonomous agents.

## 6. Safety, Monitoring and Failure Detection

- **TruthfulQA: Measuring How Models Mimic Human Falsehoods**
  Lin et al., 2022, ACL.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07958)
  Introduces a benchmark for measuring whether language models produce truthful rather than imitative false answers.

- **Detecting Hallucinations in Large Language Models Using Semantic Entropy**
  Farquhar et al., 2024, Nature.
  [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)
  Introduces semantic entropy as a method for detecting uncertainty and hallucination.

- **FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation**
  Min et al., 2023, EMNLP.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)
  Evaluates factual precision by checking atomic claims in generated text.

- **RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation**
  Es et al., 2024, EACL.
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15217)
  Provides automated metrics for evaluating retrieval-augmented generation systems.
